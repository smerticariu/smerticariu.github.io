Be honest: when macOS tells you the battery is low, do you act on it, or do you swat the
banner away and keep typing? For most people it's the second one. The built-in warnings
have become background noise.

The fix isn't *more* warnings — it's **better** ones, fired at the moments that actually
matter to you.

## Pick levels that mean something

Instead of Apple's fixed 10% and 5%, think about your real thresholds:

- **80% — unplug.** If you're limiting wear, a gentle nudge to pull the charger.
- **30% — find an outlet.** Enough runway to wrap up and relocate.
- **15% — save everything now.** A loud, unmissable warning.

In Battery Guard each of these is its own **alert rule**, and each rule can look and sound
completely different.

## Make the important one impossible to ignore

A 15% alert shouldn't look like a 30% alert. Battery Guard lets every rule have its own:

- **Banner position and size** — a small pill in the corner, or a centered card.
- **Sound** — pick a different tone per rule, or import your own.
- **Screen-edge glow** — the whole display briefly pulses in the alert color.

And for the truly critical moment, there's a **full-screen warning** across every display
that doesn't need notification permissions at all.

## Then silence the noise

Here's the trick that ties it together: once your own alerts are dialed in, macOS's
generic low-battery banners are just clutter. Battery Guard can **auto-dismiss** them the
instant they appear, so the only battery notification you ever see is the one you
designed.

The result is a system you actually respond to — because it only speaks up when it counts.
