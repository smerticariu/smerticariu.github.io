If you plug your MacBook in every night and wake up to a full charge, you're doing
exactly what your battery likes least. It's not dramatic — your Mac won't catch fire —
but over months and years, that habit measurably shortens how long your battery lasts.

## The two things lithium-ion batteries hate

Every lithium-ion cell ages faster under two conditions:

- **High state of charge.** Sitting at 100% keeps the cell at its maximum voltage, which
  accelerates the chemical reactions that permanently reduce capacity.
- **Heat.** Warmth speeds up those same reactions. A hot battery at 100% is the
  worst-case combination.

Charging to full and then keeping it there overnight hits both at once.

## What "80%" actually buys you

Holding the charge around 80% keeps the cell at a lower voltage for most of its life.
Industry testing consistently shows that a battery cycled between, say, 20% and 80%
retains noticeably more capacity after a few hundred cycles than one cycled to 100%
every time.

You give up a little runtime in exchange for a battery that stays healthier for longer —
a trade most people are happy to make on a machine that's plugged in most of the day
anyway.
